What to react against

The ticket asks for reactions, not approval. Each of these is a decision made visible — say which are wrong and the arrangement changes.

  1. Two numbers that must not look alike. “Planned per person 12,567” and “Your share 9,180” are computed by different mechanisms — headcount versus real participants. They sit on different screens with a note joining them. Is that enough, or does one of them need a different name?
  2. The reversed direction. “You pay Dad” is coloured as a warning because the cardholder owing a traveller is the surprising case. Auto-Bill could never render it. Too loud, or right?
  3. Settled is a marker, not a fact. Mum shows ✓ Settled; the balance still reads 5,240 because payments are never recorded. Adding a row involving Mum clears her marker. Does the tick oversell it?
  4. “Suggestions, not debts” as a standing panel rather than per-row wording. It has to stay true after someone pays you back. Panel, or per row?
  5. Unclaimed paid rows warn on the planned screen, where the money is double-countable — not on the split screen. Right screen?
  6. A missing rate keeps rows in NT$ and out of every THB total rather than guessing. Is being excluded from the total clear enough, or does it read as a bug?
  7. The voided row stays in the list, struck through and dimmed. On a long trip these accumulate — does it need a hide control now, or later?
  8. The claim link is a text action on the planned row (“record as actually spent →”), and a linked split row gets an accent rail. Discoverable enough?
  9. Filtering dims rather than hides. Added on your note. Auto-Bill's filter interaction is 6 of the 23 classes styled only inline, so it exists nowhere in the stylesheet — these are its real values recovered: 1.5px border, a tint fill, weight 800 when selected, 0.4 on unselected chips and 0.35 on non-matching rows. Dimming rather than hiding keeps a moved total explainable. Filters combine across Who and Category — click one in each.
  10. The accent is teal, not the house red, because the destination drives it and this is Taipei. Correct — but worth seeing, since every mockup elsewhere is red.
