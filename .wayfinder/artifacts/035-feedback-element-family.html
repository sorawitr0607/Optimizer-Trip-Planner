What to react against

Seven calls. Say which are wrong and the family changes.

  1. Four levels named Saved / Cannot / Check / Note, and the level word is the carrier. Not Success / Error / Warning / Info, because those name a severity to a developer while these name what happened to the owner. Right words?
  2. The level word is bold and coloured; there is no icon at all. Exports strip pictographs, so any glyph would vanish in the PDF. Does a bare word read fast enough when you are scanning?
  3. Disabled = 45% opacity, no shadow, plus a mandatory reason line. Losing the shadow matters in this visual language — the hard offset shadow is what makes a thing look pressable, so removing it says “not pressable” more clearly than grey does. The donor's nearest precedent is its filter dimming at 40%.
  4. Paid actions state cost on the button and spend beside it, with a meter. Three states: allowed, near the cap at US$8, refused at US$10. Only the refused one is disabled.
  5. “Requires network” is not a disabled state. The action is allowed — it just cannot succeed offline, and you may be about to reconnect. Is a reason line enough, or does it need its own visual?
  6. Confirm names the consequence instead of asking “are you sure?” Open the dialog: it says what will be destroyed and what survives, and the destructive button carries the verb, not “OK”.
  7. The missing-copy fallback is deliberately ugly (⚠ ACCESS_UNVERIFIED). It will occasionally be seen by you. That is the point — the pretty version hid 24 gaps for months.

Rejected while building

  • Toasts. They vanish, and most of these messages are conditions rather than events — a stale preview or an unverified opening time is still true in ten seconds. A banner that persists until the condition clears is the honest form.
  • A colour-only severity system. Fails the export path, fails colour-blind readers, and fails the app's own “colour is never the only signal” rule.
  • Reusing the donor's .manual-validation-panel for field errors. It reports the whole form in one block, which is why you cannot tell which field it means.
  • A generic “are you sure?” confirm. It transfers no information. Every confirm here names what is lost and what is kept.
  • Prettifying an unknown code. Tried and rejected on evidence: that is exactly the mechanism that camouflaged 24 missing English strings.
  • An alert()-style modal for status. A modal blocks the page for something the owner does not need to acknowledge, and it is on the inventory's dead-weight list.
Fidelity notes. Every element here is new; only the palette, shadows, radius and pill are lifted. Two counts in the ticket were measured lower than stated: the planner emits 68 status calls (10 success · 23 error · 15 warning · 20 info), not 59, and it disables a primary action in 10 places, not 5; the donor has 27 alert() calls, not 33. Plus Jakarta Sans and JetBrains Mono are substituted by a system stack — the woff2 files are not in the repo yet (WF-034) and the artifact runtime blocks font CDNs.
